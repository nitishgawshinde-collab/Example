DocuFlow tax assessment presenter guide

Prepared for the manager and business review of docuflow-tax-assessment.html, review build 3, September 23, 2026. This guide matches the current standalone HTML prototype. It provides a proposed slide sequence and speaker notes; it is not an existing PowerPoint deck.

Meeting objective: agree on the operating model, pilot scope, accountable owners, and requirements needed for implementation. Approval of the prototype means approval of a direction for detailed design. It does not confirm tax rules, production security, technical compatibility, or delivery estimates.

1 Demo preparation

  1. Download the latest prototype and open it in the browser you will use for the meeting. Keep this guide in a separate window or on a second screen.
  2. Rehearse on that browser before the meeting. The prototype has passed 91 logic checks using a simulated DOM; it has not passed a full browser visual or accessibility assessment.
  3. If you want a clean rehearsal, use Scope & decisions → Reset demonstration data → Reset sample workspace. This removes local demo changes. Do this only before the meeting, not halfway through a flow.
  4. Confirm the initial dataset: 14 requests, 14 fictional clients, 77 document records, and three pending scanning requests. Counts change as you work. The sample due-date and eligibility clock is September 23, 2026; audit actions use the actual system time.
  5. Select Alex Morgan in DEMO PERSONA. Use Overview to start. Save form changes before changing personas; unsaved main-form edits can trigger a discard prompt.
  6. Use seeded documents for reliable multipage demonstrations. Uploaded binaries are available only during the tab session. Do not use real client information. There are no live bank connections.
  7. For a short meeting, run slides 1–10 using the abbreviated choices below, then the decision slide. Reserve replacement and Tax Relief detail for questions.

Opening sentence: “Today we are reviewing how DocuFlow could support client Tax work across Front Office, Tax, and CDDM. This is an interactive business prototype with fictional records. I would like your feedback on responsibilities, evidence reuse, exceptions, and the decisions we need before building the production application.”

2 Proposed slides and speaker script

Use these headings as slides if you prepare a deck, or as spoken chapters while presenting the application. The full route is approximately 30 minutes before questions; optional flows can extend it to 45–60 minutes.

Slide 1 Purpose and the decision requested

Show: Overview, Alex Morgan. Time: 1 minute.

Say: “The purpose is to replace fragmented document handling and Tax work with a shared, traceable process. We want staff to see what they own, what is missing, and what must happen next. Today I am asking whether this operating model and initial scope are right, and who will own the outstanding requirements. We are not asking you to approve unconfirmed tax rules through a screen demonstration.”

Ask: “Which operational problem should the pilot improve first: returned work, repeated evidence collection, ownership visibility, or implementation delays?”

Slide 2 Shared documents and separate outcomes

Show: Assessments → Elena Moreau → Overview. Time: 2 minutes.

Say: “The record separates document readiness, Tax approval, account availability, and implementation. A document can finish verification while the business assessment continues. Tax can approve an assessment before the account becomes available. Completion should tell us which outcome has actually occurred. The CRM, TAX, CDDM, and final completion tiles illustrate the proposed team milestones.”

Boundary: The team tiles currently derive from case state. A production milestone ledger with event timestamps, reversals, and historical approvals still needs design.

Ask: “Do these outcomes match your sign-off points, and what evidence proves each one?”

Slide 3 Role specific workspaces

Show: Overview; switch through the five personas. Time: 3 minutes.

Say: “These are different views of the same saved records. Front Office sees drafts and returns, Tax sees assessments awaiting decisions, Luxembourg scanning sees paper intake, and Poland sees input or checking work. Switching persona changes dashboards, queues, counts, and actions. It does not generate a separate client dataset. If we switch while a case is open, we stay on that case and its permitted actions change.”

Boundary: The persona selector is a demonstration control, not authentication. The shared registers remain broadly visible. Personal and team queues use one sample person per role; real assignment, deputies, desk permissions, and entitlements remain to be implemented.

Slide 4 Business process library

Show: Alex → Process library. Time: 1 minute.

Say: “Users deliberately start an Account Tax Assessment, a Tax Relief request, or a service termination here. Document verification starts automatically from configured document context after intake. Future business processes can reuse the core, while retaining their own forms, routing, and decisions.”

Ask: “Which of these processes belongs in the first production release, and which countries and client types are included?”

Slide 5 BCTS checklist and evidence

Show: Amelia Bennett, TAX-2026-1081; follow Flow A steps 1–4. Time: 5 minutes.

Say: “Front Office records identification, U.S. nationality, residency, indicia, and visa information. These five question sections are distinct from the five evidence categories. Saving answers updates the illustrative evidence requirements. We then link existing accepted records by their Document IDs. The prototype blocks submission when required evidence or confirmations are missing.”

Boundary: The exact five categories and conditional mappings have not been approved. IDEN, ADDR, NATL, and DECL are specimen codes. These examples demonstrate control behavior, not an authoritative tax checklist.

Slide 6 Tax assessment and structured correction

Show: Sophie Laurent → the submitted Amelia case → Tax & implementation; use Flow A step 5. Optionally show Isabelle’s existing return with Flow B. Time: 4 minutes.

Say: “Tax reviews the linked evidence and records its decision, rationale, and implementation instructions. Field groups refer to the supplied KS and KT references, but their allowed values and mappings need validation. A return carries a reason and correction comment back to the same request. A rejection closes the request’s active work while preserving its history.”

Ask: “Who may approve, return, reject, waive a deficiency, or revise an earlier Tax decision?”

Slide 7 Implementation and independent checking

Show: Continue Amelia through Marta Kowalska and Piotr Nowak using Flow A steps 6–7. Time: 3 minutes.

Say: “Approval releases implementation only when the account is available. Data Input records the implementation reference and what was applied. A different checking persona confirms the entered values against the approved instruction. This illustrates segregation of duties. The final authorization model must be agreed with Operations and Risk.”

Boundary: Recording an OBS reference changes demo state only. The application has not written anything to OBS or verified an OBS response.

Slide 8 Multipage document review and controlled changes

Show: File cabinets → a seeded, nonarchived document, using Flow C. Time: 3 minutes.

Say: “The viewer supports multipage specimens, page navigation, zoom, and page-specific review annotations. Index-field changes are separate from original content. Editing a specimen copy creates a new document record in Processing. The accepted original remains current until the replacement passes verification. Previous case evidence links remain traceable.”

Boundary: Native PDF page navigation belongs to the browser viewer. Specimen editing does not edit real PDFs. A signature verification note is an attestation, not a legally effective e-signature.

Slide 9 Pre account dependencies

Show: Julian Hartmann, BCTS-2026-1042; Integrations; follow Flow E. Time: 2 minutes.

Say: “This assessment is Tax approved while the account is pending. When we simulate account availability, the existing request and evidence remain linked. The demo routes it back for an explicit Tax freshness review before implementation. That re-review is an assumption until the business defines the validity period and relevant change events.”

Slide 10 Tax Relief and selective termination

Show: Process library; Charlotte Weber’s existing request; Henrik Larsen’s implementation request; use the short variants in Flows F and G. Time: 3 minutes.

Say: “Tax Relief has a structured request and country evidence. An additional country gets a linked request without blindly copying the prior Tax decision. Termination targets one selected service. Other country services remain active. This keeps client context reusable while preserving separate decisions and outcomes.”

Boundary: Interactive new-business examples are limited to Denmark and Poland. BTA1 is a placeholder, BTA1–BTA9 allocation is unimplemented, and BTER’s final format remains open. Service effective dates are recorded; future-date activation and termination scheduling are not modeled.

Slide 11 Completion history and operational traceability

Show: Completion library → Oliver Hughes; Archival → Gabriel Fischer; Audit trail; Document rules. Time: 2 minutes.

Say: “Completed and rejected work appears in completion history. Archival is a separate action on a completed request. Audit history exposes the sequence of demo actions. The document rules page shows the supplied inventory of 20 Single Tax and 25 country-specific combinations. Production will need approved rule versions, retention controls, secure access, and tamper-resistant history.”

Slide 12 Decisions before implementation

Show: Scope & decisions. Time: 1 minute plus discussion.

Say: “The review should end with a scoped pilot, named business owners, and a prioritized decision register. The most urgent gaps are the evidence and tax-rule matrices, role authorities, data ownership, integration contracts, and document controls. Once those are approved, we can implement and test one complete vertical slice before extending the platform.”

Closing question: “Can we agree on the pilot process and nominate owners and target dates for these open decisions?”

6 Questions you may receive during the meeting

“Is this production ready?” “No. It validates the workflow and screen concepts. Production needs approved rules, real integrations, server-side controls, secure storage, complete audit coverage and acceptance testing.”

“Does changing persona change the client data?” “No. It changes the role’s dashboard, work queues and available actions over the same shared saved data. Production visibility will also be restricted by approved entitlements.”

“Can we reuse this in React?” “We can reuse the design language, screen structure, field definitions, sample scenarios and behavioral specifications. This HTML is not a drop-in React application. React components, Java contracts and Flowable models must be implemented and tested.”

“Are the tax rules already correct?” “Only the supplied concepts and inventory are represented. The detailed evidence, classification, country and allocation matrices need Tax SME approval. Demo values must not become production defaults.”

“Are documents actually stored in Hive or Blob?” “No. The demo stores metadata locally and temporarily holds uploaded bytes in the browser tab. Repository responsibilities and interfaces still need confirmation.”

“Can we edit or sign a real PDF here?” “We can view an uploaded PDF and record separate page-referenced notes. Editing is limited to fictional specimen copies. Legally effective signing and original PDF editing require an approved content service.”

“Does approving update OBS?” “No. The prototype records a simulated implementation and check. Production must reconcile the actual update with the approved instruction and handle failures.”

“How much faster will the process be?” “The design aims to reduce repeated entry, evidence collection and unclear handoffs. We need baseline cycle time, returns and backlog data, then a pilot to measure improvement.”

“When can we go live?” “We should estimate after the pilot scope, rule matrices, integration contracts, migration volume and platform compatibility are confirmed. The next deliverable is a scoped implementation plan with named dependencies.”

7 Recommended meeting outcome and next steps

  1. Record whether the business accepts the workflow direction, with any required changes to screens, responsibilities and milestones.
  2. Select one initial vertical slice. A candidate is individual-client BCTS from evidence selection through Tax approval, real account dependency, OBS implementation and audit. This is a proposal for approval, not an agreed scope.
  3. Assign an accountable owner and due date to every open question relevant to that slice. Record decisions with a version and approver.
  4. Produce the approved evidence/rule matrix, form and metadata dictionary, RACI, state models, interface contracts and acceptance scenarios before coding the corresponding business behavior.
  5. Validate the document viewer, repository access and Flowable integration through targeted technical spikes using approved nonproduction data.
